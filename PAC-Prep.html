Rithum's Salesforce Feedback — Service Cloud PAC, May 19 2026

Synthesized from Slack conversations, Notion project docs, and email threads across the Agentforce implementation over the past 8 months. Rithum is a B2B SaaS company (~300 person scale) using Salesforce Service Cloud as the primary support platform for two customer bases (Retailers and Suppliers) through an active ServiceNow → Salesforce migration. Agentforce is a strategic H2 2026 priority.

Salesforce Products in UseService Cloud, Agentforce, Data Cloud, Einstein AI, MIAW (Live Chat)
Current PhaseActive ServiceNow migration; Agentforce internal agent in sandbox; external agent planned H2
TeamJordan Christensen, Cole Garrow (Support Ops), Matt Schultz (SF Admin), Liz Meunier (SF Admin)

Theme 1 · Agentforce Architecture
The 2-Field Relevance Limit Is the Biggest Blocker to Knowledge Agent Quality
Agentforce can only scan two article fields (Title + Short Description) to determine whether a KB article is relevant—severely limiting answer quality for any article where the key content lives in the body.
▾
What We're Experiencing

Our Agentforce knowledge agent produces inconsistent results that appear directly tied to what information is in the Title and Short Description fields vs. the article body. When a question's answer lives in the body of an article but isn't echoed in the two indexed fields, the agent either returns nothing or returns a tangentially related article with a generic "I couldn't find specific information" response.

The agent works well for articles where the title closely mirrors how a user would phrase the question. It fails—sometimes completely—when the user's terminology, the article title, and the actual content diverge even slightly.

Specific Examples from Our Sandbox
  • "How do you submit pricing updates for SKUs?" → Excellent, detailed multi-step response with correct source citations. Notion · AgentForce April doc
  • "How do I resolve order exceptions?" → Answered well. Slack · #p_agentforce-service-agent-imp
  • "What are the requirements for connecting to AS2?" → "I couldn't find specific details." The AS2 URL is clearly documented in the KB. Notion · AgentForce April doc
  • "How do I process orders?" → "I couldn't find specific information on processing orders." Rephrased as "How do I ship orders?" → Full correct answer. Notion · AgentForce April doc
  • Instructions for uploading items to ACS — fails to find despite a well-written article existing. Slack · Cole Garrow, Apr 13
Our Ask
  • 1
    Allow configurable field selection per Knowledge record type. We want to point Agentforce at the "Title" and "Procedure Body" fields for task-type articles, and "Title" and "Concept Body" for concept articles, rather than a one-size-fits-all Title + Short Description. Jordan raised this directly with the team in April 2026.
  • 2
    Increase the relevance index to include a third field or a full-text AI summary field. Our workaround (adding a hidden AI-generated summary field to each article) is effective but requires significant KB maintenance overhead. A native solution would be preferable.
  • 3
    Improve semantic/synonym matching. The agent should understand that "process orders" and "ship orders" likely refer to the same workflow and surface articles accordingly.
Theme 2 · Agentforce Architecture
The 5-Source Ceiling and Attachment Blindness Create Structural Gaps
Two additional hard limits—a 5-article source cap and inability to read KB attachments—compound the retrieval quality issues, particularly for a B2B platform with complex technical documentation.
▾
What We're Experiencing

Many real-world support questions require synthesizing information from more than 5 articles. Broad operational questions like "how do I cancel an order?" may have answers spread across multiple articles covering different retailer-specific variations. The 5-source limit means the agent gives an incomplete answer without signaling that more sources exist.

Our Knowledge Base also stores critical technical specifications—EDI specs, retailer-specific requirements, integration guides—as PDF or image attachments within articles. Agentforce cannot read these attachments at all, which makes the agent nearly useless for a significant category of technical questions our support team handles daily.

Evidence
  • Salesforce Support acknowledged the 5-source limit and suggested making the KB "more expansive and more concise"—our team noted this is a significant undertaking misaligned with our rollout timeline. Slack · Cole Garrow, Apr 13
  • Retailer pages and EDI specifications are stored as PDF attachments; Agentforce cannot read them regardless of how the question is phrased. Notion · AgentForce April doc
  • Our workaround: convert screenshot content to plaintext and store in hidden-from-customer article fields. Works but is unsustainable at scale. Notion · AgentForce April doc
Our Ask
  • 1
    Raise the source ceiling or provide a configurable limit based on question complexity. At minimum, surface a signal to the agent that it may have an incomplete answer when the source limit is hit.
  • 2
    Enable attachment reading for common formats (PDF, DOCX) within Knowledge articles. This is a high-value capability for B2B support organizations with spec-heavy documentation.
Theme 3 · Implementation Experience
Implementation Velocity Is Too Slow—Even for Motivated, Resourced Teams
Rithum ran a formal Agentforce for Service POC with Salesforce (fall 2025), has dedicated admin capacity, and is actively prioritized on Agentforce—and we're still not in production 8 months later. The iteration loop is too long.
▾
What We're Experiencing

Our Salesforce admin team has been the primary bottleneck—not through lack of effort, but because each configuration change requires navigating the Data Cloud sandbox, deployment via Gearset, and coordination with the broader SF admin team's competing priorities. The admin noted they had only been able to dedicate 4–6 hours per month to Agentforce improvement over a recent stretch, despite it being a stated priority.

Jordan escalated this in April 2026, stating: "I need to be able to go faster with this Agentforce deployment so that we can iterate faster and get to a usable solution." The gap between the sandbox POC and production readiness has stretched from Q1 to H2 2026—a 6+ month slip from original targets.

Root Causes Identified
  • Data Cloud sandbox is volatile (daily changes from multiple teams + Perficient); difficult to safely make iterative improvements. Slack · RJ Yenesel, Apr 29
  • Salesforce Support's own Agentforce implementation guidance was limited—the team noted SF Support is "similar" (equally unfamiliar) in early sessions. Notion · AgentForce April doc
  • Agentforce configuration requires deep admin expertise that's in short supply and shared across competing initiatives. Slack · Matt Schultz, Apr 30
  • No lightweight "iterate without an admin" path exists for testing prompt changes and KB field configurations. Slack · Jordan Christensen, Apr 29
  • Agentforce requires changing our Knowledge Base, Processes and Salesforce instance data structure (the "road") to accommodate the Agent (the "vehicle"), rather than the other way around. Slack · Matthew Schultz, May 15
Our Ask
  • 1
    Provide a lightweight iteration environment that doesn't require Gearset deployment or Data Cloud sync for prompt template and field configuration changes. A "quick test" layer for agent configuration would dramatically accelerate iteration.
  • 2
    Invest in implementation support quality. Agentforce specialist guidance needs to extend to practical "here's the exact configuration for your use case" level, not just directional advice.
  • 3
    Clearer Trailhead paths for new Agentforce admins. When Jordan tried to get a new admin up to speed quickly (April 2026), there was no clear "beginner + advanced" learning sequence for Agentforce for Service specifically.
Theme 4 · Agent Configuration
Internal vs. External Agent Behavior Should Be Separately Configurable Without Duplicating Everything
Default agent behaviors—like "escalate to a live agent" prompts—are appropriate for external agents but disruptive in internal rep-facing deployments. There's no easy way to fork these behaviors without rebuilding the agent.
▾
What We're Experiencing

During our sandbox testing, Jordan noted that once the internal agent got stuck, it would recommend the user "escalate this to a live person"—a default behavior appropriate for an external customer-facing agent, but counterproductive when the user is the support rep. The agent should understand its deployment context.

More broadly, our roadmap calls for building both an internal rep-assist agent and an external client-facing deflection agent. The configuration overhead of maintaining two separate agents with largely overlapping content but different behavioral rules adds significant admin burden.

Evidence
  • Jordan flagged the "escalate to live person" behavior directly: "Probably not necessary for this internal one. Would probably be good to remove that option if we can." Slack · Jordan Christensen, Mar 27
  • Cole suggested the external agent proactively state what it can answer at the start of a conversation to set client expectations—a configuration capability not clearly available today. Slack · Cole Garrow, Feb 27
  • Alex Brown raised a specific security concern: preventing the agent from accidentally disclosing sensitive customer data to the wrong party in a multi-tenant environment. Slack · Alex Brown, Feb 27
Our Ask
  • 1
    Support an "Internal / Employee" deployment mode that suppresses customer-facing default behaviors (live agent escalation prompts, apology language, etc.) out of the box.
  • 2
    Built-in PII guardrails for case data context. As we expand to use case email messages as Agentforce context, we need confidence that PII is redacted before being surfaced in agent responses.
  • 3
    Agent "scope declaration" capability — ability for an external agent to proactively communicate what topic areas it can and can't answer at the start of an interaction, to set client expectations and reduce frustration.
  • 4
    External Pitfall Visibility: We need better visibility to external (client facing) Agentforce pitfalls and mitigation methods being suggested.
Theme 5 · Strategic Roadmap
The Full Case Lifecycle AI Vision Is Real—We Need Roadmap Clarity to Plan Against It
Rithum's H2 2026 roadmap depends on Agentforce for case classification, AI-generated summaries, next-best-action, and response drafting. We need to know what's production-ready, what's coming, and when.
▾
Our Strategic Vision

Our AI roadmap (Project Polaris) maps out a full case lifecycle AI deployment in H2 2026, contingent on the ServiceNow → Salesforce migration completing by June. This includes: (1) L0 external deflection agent for client-facing case reduction; (2) internal rep-assist agent with KB + case data context; (3) case classification and auto-routing; (4) AI-generated case summaries on close; (5) next-best-action recommendations; and (6) AI-assisted email response drafting.

We're currently stalled at step (2) in the pre-production sandbox after 8+ months. The gap between the Agentforce marketing vision and what's reliably achievable by a well-resourced but not deeply specialized team is significant—and we need honest clarity on which of these capabilities are genuinely production-ready vs. aspirational.

Evidence & Context
  • Agentforce email drafting described as "just not good" and "too context limited" in our internal testing—occasionally produces a usable starter template but not reliable enough for deployment. Slack · Cole Garrow, Feb 27
  • AI-generated case summaries and next-best-action are named items in our 2026 Strategy doc but have no implementation plan yet—dependency on production deployment of the base agent. Notion · AI Opportunity Map, Apr 30
  • Product signal extraction from Salesforce cases is a key strategic goal—we want to use unified case history as structured product feedback input. Notion · AI Opportunity Map, Apr 30
Our Ask
  • 1
    Publish a clear "what's production-ready today" guide for Agentforce for Service, with realistic expectations on what a ~10-person support org can achieve vs. a large enterprise with dedicated resources.
  • 2
    Email response drafting roadmap. The current capability is insufficient for deployment. What does the improvement path look like and on what timeline?
  • 3
    Native case clustering / product signal extraction. We're planning to build a weekly AI scan of closed cases to surface product themes. Is this something Salesforce plans to offer natively, or is it always a custom build?
Theme 6 · What's Working
Where Service Cloud & Agentforce Are Genuinely Delivering Value
Not all feedback is a pain point. There are specific capabilities and partnership moments that have meaningfully moved us forward.
▾
  • When KB articles are well-structured and terminology-matched, Agentforce produces excellent, citation-backed step-by-step answers. The pricing updates and order exception examples were impressive.
  • The versatility with Data libraries, Prompt Templates, Agent Actions, and Agent Instructions is promising.
  • All presentations and guides provided for the implantation of Agentforce have been provided in a manageable and intuitive way.
  • The Salesforce Agentforce team was clutch with getting us over the oversimplification hump[cite: 11]. It is better understood that Case, Knowledge Base and related record access is important for getting robust and complete outputs[cite: 12].
  • The KB article → case association feature (attaching articles to cases to build context over time) is a smart long-term capability we're actively adopting.
  • The Salesforce AE and Agentforce specialist engagement (Jack Seminara, Thomas, Jamisen) has been responsive and genuinely helpful when we've escalated specific configuration questions.
  • Using Anthropic Claude Sonnet 4 as the prompt template model for "Answer Questions with Knowledge" has been a positive choice—response quality is noticeably better than the default model we tested earlier.
  • The Salesforce Admin team's Gearset-based deployment pipeline is rigorous and well-governed, even if it slows iteration speed.
  • Service Cloud's omni-channel routing and case management foundations are solid—the migration from ServiceNow is building on a reliable base.
  • The Agentforce for Service POC program (fall 2025) was a valuable investment that gave us a head start on understanding the platform's architecture before committing to production.
